Limits

Exploration tool, not a detection. SPHEREx frames from different dates also differ in wavelength, so a brightness change alone is not proof of motion: Blink only pairs frames within 0.05 µm, more than 1 h apart. The ice readout is a quick look (3×3 median minus field median, 3σ), not a calibrated product. Data: NASA/IPAC IRSA, SPHEREx QR3.

How to read this

What is this? SPHEREx is a NASA space telescope photographing the whole sky in infrared light, split into about 100 colours. This page shows its real images of one spot of sky.

1 · Pick a target on the left (or type coordinates / a name in CMD). Images come from NASA's archive, which can take a minute.

2 · Step through the images with ‹ › or Play. Each one is the same spot at a different colour (µm) and date, shown at the bottom.

3 · Open the Spectrum tab (top, or press 2): brightness at the crosshair for each colour. Dips at 3.05, 4.27 and 4.67 µm mean ice (water, CO₂, CO), where planets and comets form.

  • Blink flips between dates to spot things that move or change
  • Diff subtracts the previous image
  • Gaia / WISE mark known stars and infrared sources
  • Sky tab: click anywhere on the map to look there
